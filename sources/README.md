# Primary Sources

Primary-source documents obtained during research for this petition, kept here so the citations in `PETITION.md` can be checked directly against the actual filings rather than secondary summaries.

| File | What it is | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| [`1997-04-01_FCC-97-99_R-and-O_Special-Event-Call-Sign-System.pdf`](./1997-04-01_FCC-97-99_R-and-O_Special-Event-Call-Sign-System.pdf) | FCC 97-99, Report and Order, WT Docket No. 95-57, 12 FCC Rcd 3804 (adopted Mar. 20, 1997; released Apr. 1, 1997) | Petition ¶¶ 2, 11, and 24 and footnotes 3 and 10. ¶¶ 12–16 create the special event call sign system and delegate the coordinator database; ¶ 16 is the scarcity rationale quoted in the Petition and also finds a larger block unnecessary, which ¶ 24 addresses. Its note citing the 1995 vanity order (adopted Dec. 23, 1994, 10 FCC Rcd 1039, at ¶ 13) confirms footnote 2. |
| [`2010-11-08_FCC-10-189_R-and-O_Vanity-and-Club-Call-Signs.pdf`](./2010-11-08_FCC-10-189_R-and-O_Vanity-and-Club-Call-Signs.pdf) | FCC 10-189, Report and Order, WT Docket No. 09-209, 25 FCC Rcd 16351 (adopted Nov. 2, 2010; released Nov. 8, 2010) | Petition ¶ 13 and footnote 12. The order never mentions special event call signs, confirming that ARRL's 2010 special event suggestion went unaddressed; ¶¶ 33–34 decline to add sequential call signs because about fifteen million were available. |
| [`2025-02_BNetzA_Antrag-Sonderrufzeichen-4-7-stellig.pdf`](./2025-02_BNetzA_Antrag-Sonderrufzeichen-4-7-stellig.pdf) | Bundesnetzagentur, application form for a 4-to-7-character special call sign (Ref. 225, Feb. 2025) | Petition Table 1 (Germany) and footnote 30. Shows the Sonderrufzeichen is assigned as a club station call sign, for at most one year, and cannot be extended. |
| [`1999-04-30_FCC-99-84_MOO_Popkin-Reconsideration.pdf`](./1999-04-30_FCC-99-84_MOO_Popkin-Reconsideration.pdf) | FCC 99-84, Memorandum Opinion and Order, WT Docket No. 95-57 (adopted Apr. 26, 1999; released Apr. 30, 1999) | Petition § III.C, ¶ 16 and footnotes 16–17 (¶ 4 of the order: no complaints about unavailable call signs); also § VI, ¶ 24 and footnote 37 (¶¶ 4–5), and ¶ 28 and footnote 49, citing ¶ 6 of the order for the Commission's description of the coordinators' role. Denies a petition for reconsideration by David B. Popkin seeking only administrative changes to the special event call sign system (identification timing, duration/reuse limits, coordinator compensation) — confirms it never addressed call sign format or length and is not adverse to this Petition. |
| [`2010-03-26_ARRL-Comments_WT-Docket-09-209.pdf`](./2010-03-26_ARRL-Comments_WT-Docket-09-209.pdf) | Comments of ARRL, the National Association for Amateur Radio, WT Docket No. 09-209 (filed Mar. 26, 2010) | Petition § III.C, ¶ 13 and footnote 11. ¶ 22 of this filing is the primary-source basis for this Petition's central historical claim: ARRL itself recommended expanding the special event call sign format beyond 1x1, citing the same ITU RR No. 19.68A this Petition relies on. |
| [`2012-01-11_FCC-12-1_MOO_ARRL-Reconsideration-Denied.pdf`](./2012-01-11_FCC-12-1_MOO_ARRL-Reconsideration-Denied.pdf) | FCC 12-1, Memorandum Opinion and Order, WT Docket No. 09-209 (adopted Jan. 3, 2012; released Jan. 11, 2012) | Petition § III.C, ¶¶ 14-15 and footnotes 13-15. Denies ARRL's Jan. 2011 petition for reconsideration of FCC 10-189 (on unrelated club-station vanity call sign limits) and expressly terminates WT Docket No. 09-209 (¶ 10) — the basis for this Petition's "no other procedural vehicle exists" argument. Footnote 10 of this order also shows the Commission separately declined a different ARRL request about sequential/vanity format availability generally, distinguished in Petition ¶ 15. |

FCC 99-84, ARRL's 2010 comments, and FCC 12-1 were supplied directly by the petitioner during drafting and should still be spot-checked against ECFS/docs.fcc.gov before filing. FCC 97-99 and FCC 10-189 were downloaded from docs.fcc.gov, and the Bundesnetzagentur form from bundesnetzagentur.de, on Sept. 16, 2026.
