# Primary Sources

Primary-source documents obtained during research for this petition, kept here so the citations in `PETITION.md` can be checked directly against the actual filings rather than secondary summaries.

| File | What it is | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| [`1999-04-30_FCC-99-84_MOO_Popkin-Reconsideration.pdf`](./1999-04-30_FCC-99-84_MOO_Popkin-Reconsideration.pdf) | FCC 99-84, Memorandum Opinion and Order, WT Docket No. 95-57 (adopted Apr. 26, 1999; released Apr. 30, 1999) | Petition § III.C, ¶ 13 and footnote 15. Denies a petition for reconsideration by David B. Popkin seeking only administrative changes to the special event call sign system (identification timing, duration/reuse limits, coordinator compensation) — confirms it never addressed call sign format or length and is not adverse to this Petition. |
| [`2010-03-26_ARRL-Comments_WT-Docket-09-209.pdf`](./2010-03-26_ARRL-Comments_WT-Docket-09-209.pdf) | Comments of ARRL, the National Association for Amateur Radio, WT Docket No. 09-209 (filed Mar. 26, 2010) | Petition § III.C, ¶ 10 and footnote 10. ¶ 22 of this filing is the primary-source basis for this Petition's central historical claim: ARRL itself recommended expanding the special event call sign format beyond 1x1, citing the same ITU RR No. 19.68A this Petition relies on. |
| [`2012-01-11_FCC-12-1_MOO_ARRL-Reconsideration-Denied.pdf`](./2012-01-11_FCC-12-1_MOO_ARRL-Reconsideration-Denied.pdf) | FCC 12-1, Memorandum Opinion and Order, WT Docket No. 09-209 (adopted Jan. 3, 2012; released Jan. 11, 2012) | Petition § III.C, ¶¶ 11-12 and footnotes 12-14. Denies ARRL's Jan. 2011 petition for reconsideration of FCC 10-189 (on unrelated club-station vanity call sign limits) and expressly terminates WT Docket No. 09-209 (¶ 10) — the basis for this Petition's "no other procedural vehicle exists" argument. Footnote 10 of this order also shows the Commission separately declined a different ARRL request about sequential/vanity format availability generally, distinguished in Petition ¶ 12. |

All three files were supplied directly by the petitioner during drafting; none were independently re-downloaded by an AI agent, so their authenticity should still be spot-checked against ECFS/docs.fcc.gov before filing.
